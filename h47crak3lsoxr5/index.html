Customers

Everyone in your rewards program: what they told us, what their tickets say, and what to put in front of them next. Contact details and marketing tools follow each person's permissions.

Item file

One item master · per-store stock, cost and price

Descriptions, units and network prices are set once for every store. Each store keeps its own on hand, average cost and, when the market calls for it, its own retail price. On hand is always the sum of that store's ledger.

Purchasing

A PO is how stock gets into the system, however the vendor heard about the order: EDI, email, a phone call, the rep, their website, or a truck that's already here. Build it from the vendor's quote, a paste, low stock or by keying it. Credit POs send stock back to a vendor and set up the credit we're owed.

Payables

Every store in your scope

Vendor invoices land here from EDI, the document inbox and phone photos. AI reads the paperwork, fixed rules check it against the PO and what was actually received, and a person approves anything that moves money.

Transfers

A store manager builds a list and ships it; the receiving store's manager counts it and accepts it. Stock rides in transit in between, and each store's books balance on their own the whole way, using an interstore clearing account that nets to zero across the network.

Admin · Roles and security

Every individual process on every screen is its own switch: On, Needs approval, or Off. Some also carry a limit. Each person also has a store scope (one store, a region, or all stores), and nothing outside that scope is reachable or approvable by them.

Yardstick is a working name. Everything here runs in this page with made-up stores, items, customers, costs and people so the flow can be tried end to end. Three stores stand in for sixty: stores, regions and scopes are plain records, so adding store 60 means adding a row, not changing code. In the real system every permission and store-scope check also runs on the server. Refreshing the page starts everything over.
